Zeta — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026 · Version 1.4

In short

Zeta has no server. None of your data reaches us, because there is nowhere for it to go. There is no advertising network in the app, no analytics, no crash reporting and no account. Everything it reads — your contacts, your messages, your pictures, your notes — stays on your phone.

The only things that leave are the smallest pieces needed to do something you asked for: approximate location for the weather, a track name for lyrics, what you type for search suggestions, the topics and sites you chose for the news. Each one is listed below.

What stays on the phone

None of this leaves your device. Every permission is asked for only when the part of the app that needs it is used; refuse one and that part stops working, the rest carries on.

About the keyboard in particular: nothing is learned from password fields, the "do not learn from this" flag apps set (private browsing, one-time codes) is honoured, and a clip a password manager has marked sensitive is never written to the clipboard history.

What leaves the phone

Each of these is optional and happens only when you use that feature. Nothing carries your identity, your device id or an advertising id.

Weather — open-meteo.com Your approximate location (latitude/longitude, rounded to about a kilometre), or the name of a city you searched for. No account, nothing identifying.

Lyrics and album art — lrclib.net, musicbrainz.org, coverartarchive.org, itunes.apple.com Only if you switched it on in settings. What is sent is the title, artist and album of the track playing. Your file is never uploaded.

Search suggestions — the search engine you chose (Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yandex) What you type in the browser is sent as you type, to suggest completions. It can be switched off in settings, and then nothing is sent.

Email — the servers you enter The app connects straight to your IMAP/SMTP server. Nothing sits in between, and there is no server of ours.

Cloud files — the app connects to none itself When you open a file from the cloud in the Files hub, or save one there, Android's own file picker is used: the cloud app on your phone (Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox…) brings it or takes it. The app signs in to no cloud account and keeps no account details.

News — news.google.com and the sites you follow Only when the news hub is opened, or while its tile is on Start. The feeds of the topics you chose come from Google News, in the edition of your phone's language and country; the RSS feeds of the sites you follow come straight from those sites. When you open a story, its picture and summary may be asked of the story's own page. No location, nothing identifying.

Ad and tracker blocklist — raw.githubusercontent.com If blocking is on in the browser, Steven Black's open blocklist is downloaded once, then weekly and only on Wi-Fi. The file is fetched; nothing is sent.

The browser Sites you visit are between you and them. The app does not sit in the middle and does not report anywhere. With blocking on, the requests pages make to advertising and tracking servers are stopped on the phone. The icons on the start page's site tiles are asked of Google's icon service by the site's name. "Translate page" opens the page through Google Translate.

Between your phone and your TV (the phone link)

Zeta also runs on a television. If you pair your phone with a TV on the same Wi-Fi network, the phone tells that TV about incoming calls and messages, and the TV shows them in a corner of the screen. Nothing is sent unless you pair the two.

  • What goes: the caller's or sender's name (as in your contacts, or the number), the kind of notice (call, missed call, message), the name of the app a message came from and your phone's name. The text of a message is not sent by default; only if you turn on "send the message text too" on the phone do its first 300 characters go.
  • Where it goes: only to the TV you paired, directly over your local network. There is no server in between; it does not go out to the internet and never reaches us.
  • How it is protected: pairing uses a one-time six-digit code shown on the TV; every notice is encrypted (AES-GCM) with a key only those two devices hold, protected by the Android Keystore.
  • How it is read: while Zeta is your default phone and SMS app it sees calls and texts itself; otherwise it reads them from your phone and messaging apps' notifications (this needs notification access). Messages from other apps such as WhatsApp are, if you choose, read from their notifications too.
  • On the TV: a notice is only shown, disappears after a few seconds and is not stored. The TV keeps only the names and keys of the phones paired with it.
  • Turning it off: on the phone, settings → connections → TV link turns each kind off, or all of it; a paired device can be removed from either side.

On a television

If you turn on the accessibility service on a TV, it is used for three things only: catching the remote's Home button, opening the TV's power menu or putting it to sleep when you choose that in the TV interface's shutdown panel, and showing a paired phone's notice over other apps. Nothing on the screen is read, and nothing is recorded or sent.

Where it is kept

All of it stays in the app's own private storage, on your phone.

  • Email passwords and cloud tokens: in a vault, AES-GCM, under a key held by the Android Keystore. The key never leaves the phone and is never backed up.
  • Locked notes: the body is encrypted the same way. Even with root access to the phone, a locked note cannot be read.
  • Browsing history and downloads: in a file of their own, excluded from backup.
  • The reading list, its offline copies and where you left each video: in files of their own, excluded from backup.

Backup

If Android's backup and device-transfer are on, the app's appearance settings and Start screen layout are included. These are deliberately excluded and never leave the phone: email accounts and cached messages, notes and their attachments, the keyboard's learned words and clipboard history, cloud accounts, calendar entries, browsing history, the reading list, where you left each video, the location you watch the weather for, and the password vault.

The Pro trial. When you start the 10-day trial, the moment it began (a date, nothing else) is kept in the app's settings, in Google Play services' Block Store and, as a day, in the component settings Android keeps for the app; the last is so that clearing the app's data does not start the trial again, and it goes when the app is uninstalled. With the phone's backup on, the date survives an uninstall and reinstall; with end-to-end encrypted backup it moves to a new phone with your Google account's backup. Nothing identifying you, your account or your device is attached, and none of it comes to us.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no age information.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling removes everything the app kept. To clear it without uninstalling: Android Settings → Apps → Zeta → Storage → Clear data. There is no account or record of yours on our side to delete, because none is ever created.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date above is updated and the change is mentioned in the app's "what's new" screen.
